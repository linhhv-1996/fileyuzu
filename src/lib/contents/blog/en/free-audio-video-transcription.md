## What Is Audio and Video Transcription?

Transcription means converting spoken words into written text.

If the speech is inside an audio file, it is audio transcription. If the speech is inside a video file, it is video transcription.

For example:

```text
meeting.mp3 → meeting transcript
webinar.mp4 → webinar transcript
interview.m4a → interview transcript
lecture.mov → lecture transcript
voice-note.wav → voice note transcript
```

The transcript can then be copied, edited, searched, summarized, translated, or turned into other content.

A transcript is not always perfect, and it is not the same as a polished article, meeting note, or subtitle file. Think of it as a useful raw draft that saves you from typing everything manually.

## Why Choosing the Spoken Language Matters

Language selection is not a small detail. It directly affects the quality of the transcript.

If your file is in English but you choose another language, the output may look strange. If your file is in Japanese but you leave the wrong language selected, the transcript may miss words or produce broken text.

Before starting transcription, check:

* What language is the speaker using?
* Is the whole file in one language?
* Are there short foreign phrases inside a mostly single-language recording?
* Is the audio mainly English, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Spanish, French, Vietnamese, Portuguese, or another supported language?

For mixed-language files, choose the main spoken language and expect to review the transcript manually.

## What Free Transcription Is Not Perfect For

It is also important to be realistic.

Free transcription is not magic. You should not expect a raw transcript to be perfect in every situation.

You may need manual cleanup when the file has:

* noisy background audio
* overlapping speakers
* strong echo
* quiet speech
* multiple languages
* music under speech
* uncommon names
* technical terms
* legal or medical content
* poor microphone quality

For important documents, treat the transcript as a draft and proofread it carefully.

## Tips for Better Audio and Video Transcription

### Select the Correct Spoken Language

This is the first thing to check. The tool requires language selection before transcription. Do not assume it will automatically detect the language.

### Use Clear Recordings

The cleaner the speech, the better the transcript. Use files where the speaker is close to the microphone and background noise is low.

### Avoid Overlapping Speakers

When people talk over each other, transcription becomes harder. For future recordings, ask speakers to take turns if possible.

### Trim Unneeded Sections

Remove long silence, music intros, countdowns, or irrelevant parts before transcribing if you can. This makes the transcript easier to review.

### Use Original Files When Possible

Files sent through messaging apps may be compressed. If you have the original MP3, WAV, M4A, MP4, or MOV file, use it.

### Proofread Important Text

Always check names, numbers, dates, product terms, acronyms, and quotes. These are common places where transcription can need correction.

## A Simple Post-Transcription Workflow

Getting the transcript is only the first step. Here is a practical workflow that works for most files.

### 1. Scan the Transcript

Read quickly and remove obvious junk, repeated filler, long silence notes, or broken fragments.

### 2. Fix Important Terms

Correct names, brands, product terms, acronyms, numbers, dates, and anything that will be reused.

### 3. Add Structure

Break the text into sections. Add headings if needed.

### 4. Extract the Useful Parts

Pull out action items, quotes, ideas, decisions, questions, or explanations.

### 5. Create the Final Output

Turn the transcript into the format you actually need: notes, summary, article, email, documentation, study guide, or content draft.

This is where transcription becomes valuable. The raw text saves time, but the cleaned version is what you can actually use.
